# Heuristics and Gaps

Turn a meeting transcript into two things: teachable decision heuristics that an adaptive tutor can deliver to a learner, and a list of knowledge gaps that become the questions for the next interview.

## Purpose

Experts rarely say their rules out loud. They show them when they make a decision, wave off a suggestion, correct someone, or say something like  "obviously." Your job is to recover those rules in a form a tutor can teach and test. You must also be honest about which rules you actually recovered and which ones you only gleaned.

A **heuristic** is a reusable judgment, written as:

> When **[situation]**, **[action]**, because **[reason]**.

A line from the transcript qualifies only if it passes the **transfer test**: it would change what a learner does in a situation the transcript never discussed. The following fail the transfer test and are not heuristics:

- facts ("Gateway speaks the OpenAI format")
- logistics ("put your prompt in your student folder")
- plans and announcements ("there will be three products")
- descriptions of how something works, unless they carry a judgment about why it was built that way

A **gap** is a place where the transcript shows that knowledge exists but does not contain it. A gap is not a failure of the extraction. It is half of the deliverable.

## Inputs and limits

- Input: one transcript, pasted or given as a path. If none is provided, reply `Waiting for a transcript.` and stop.
- Use only the transcript. Do not open other files, search, browse, or use outside knowledge about the people, products, or technologies mentioned. If a speaker refers to a document or file, that reference is a possible gap, not something to look up.
- Every speaker is in scope. Every subject is in scope, including technical design, product and business, teaching and learning, and team process. Do not rank one domain above another.

## Evidence tiers

Every heuristic carries exactly one tier.

| Tier | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `STATED` | A speaker said the rule. The reason may or may not have been said. |
| `DEMONSTRATED` | A speaker acted on the rule in the meeting (made a decision, redirected, corrected, refused) without stating it as a rule. |
| `INFERRED` | A pattern visible across two or more separate moments, never said as a rule and never acted on in a single clear decision. |

These tiers come with four rules:

1. An inference supported by only one moment is not a heuristic. Record it as a gap.
2. If the reason was never given, write `because: not stated` and open a linked gap. Never supply a reason yourself. A tutor must not teach a rationale the expert did not give.
3. If two heuristics conflict (between speakers, or within one speaker), keep both, link them, and open a gap that asks which one wins and when.
4. Tiers describe evidence, not importance. Do not add confidence numbers.

## The passes

Work through the passes in order. Each pass produces a deliverable that the next pass consumes. Do not show the intermediate deliverables in the final output unless a pass says to.

### Pass 1: Read

Break the transcript into turns (`@Speaker [m:ss]: text`). Join a speaker's consecutive turns when a sentence runs across a timestamp break. Transcripts generated by speech recognition will mishear names and terms. Build a short list of terms as heard, with the normalized form you will use, but only where the context makes the intended word clear. If two readings are plausible, keep the term as heard and note both.

*Deliverable:* the list of turns, and the terms-as-heard list.

### Pass 2: Sort

Label each substantive turn with one of the following:

- `JUDGMENT`: a choice, preference, refusal, correction, priority, or evaluation
- `FRICTION`: a question, misunderstanding, pushback, or correction between speakers. These are the moments where one person's assumption became visible to another.
- `DEFERRAL`: the speaker postponed or redirected instead of answering ("look in this file", "I have no idea", "we'll figure it out", "it's not that complicated")
- `OTHER`: facts, logistics, plans, pleasantries, and descriptions with no judgment. These are set aside.

*Deliverable:* the labeled turns. Only `JUDGMENT`, `FRICTION`, and `DEFERRAL` go forward.

### Pass 3: Extract heuristics

For each `JUDGMENT` and `FRICTION` turn, ask what rule would have produced this choice or this reaction. Write the rule in the When / action / because form. Then generalize the situation until it would apply outside this meeting, but stop before it becomes a platitude. "When building anything, be careful" has been generalized too far. Merge turns that express the same rule, and keep all of their timestamps.

Assign a tier. Record the speaker. Quote the shortest fragment of the transcript that carries the evidence, exactly as heard, with `[...]` replacing profanity.

*Deliverable:* draft heuristics with tier, speaker, timestamps, and evidence fragments.

### Pass 4: Test

Put each draft heuristic through the following checks, in order:

1. **Transfer.** Does it change a decision in a new situation? If not, delete it.
2. **Restatement.** Is the rule just the quote reworded? If the speaker said the full rule including the reason, keep it as `STATED`. That is still teachable. If your version adds nothing the quote doesn't already say and the quote is a fact rather than a judgment, delete it.
3. **Support.** Does an `INFERRED` heuristic have two or more separate moments behind it? If not, move it to the gaps.
4. **Counter-evidence.** Does anything in the transcript contradict it? If yes, record the conflict and open a gap.
5. **Invented reasons.** Did the reason come from the transcript? If not, replace it with `not stated` and open a gap.

*Deliverable:* the surviving heuristics.

### Pass 5: Find gaps

Collect gaps from the following sources:

- every `DEFERRAL` turn
- every heuristic marked `because: not stated`
- every single-moment inference moved out of Pass 4
- every conflict between heuristics
- every term or concept a speaker relied on but never explained, where the learner would need it
- every instruction that is ambiguous or self-contradictory as transcribed

For each gap, write an interview question that would draw out a **story**, not a definition. Ask about a specific time, a choice made under pressure, or what went wrong when the rule was not followed. Add one follow-up variation that changes a single condition of that story ("What if it had been...?"). Gaps that block the most heuristics come first.

*Deliverable:* the gaps, each linked to the heuristics it blocks.

### Pass 6: Build teaching units

Turn each surviving heuristic into a unit a tutor can deliver:

- **Prerequisites:** the concepts a learner must already understand. Draw these from a concept list built only from terms the transcript uses. If a prerequisite concept is never explained in the transcript, link it to a gap.
- **Practice scenario:** a new situation, not one from the transcript, in which the heuristic decides the right action. Keep it to two or three sentences.
- **Expected reasoning:** what a learner who has absorbed the heuristic would do, and why, in terms of the heuristic.
- **Likely wrong answer:** the most tempting mistake. If a `FRICTION` moment in the transcript shows someone actually making this mistake, cite it and mark it `observed`. Otherwise mark it `predicted`.

Order the units so that no unit appears before its prerequisites.

*Deliverable:* the ordered teaching units.

### Pass 7: Check and write

Before writing, confirm all of the following:

- every heuristic has a tier, at least one timestamp, and an evidence fragment
- no reason appears that the transcript does not contain
- no `INFERRED` heuristic rests on a single moment
- no practice scenario is copied from the transcript
- every gap links to at least one heuristic or concept, and every `not stated` links to a gap
- no outside knowledge was used

Then write the output below and nothing else.
